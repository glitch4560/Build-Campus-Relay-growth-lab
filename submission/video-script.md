# Three-minute walkthrough

AI-assisted script. Rehearse and record it in your own voice. Suggested duration: approximately three minutes; pauses and your speaking pace affect timing. Show the actual prototype while recording. Do not imply that the simulated registrations are campaign results.

## 0:00-0:30 - The student and the decision

“My goal is 500 final-year engineering registrations in seven days with two thousand rupees. I am targeting students who have watched AI tutorials but still want a small project they can explain in an interview. That student need is a hypothesis; I would test it with five short conversations before launch.

My bet is trusted campus distribution. I would recruit twenty class or club leads across ten colleges, then give each one a tracked link and a concrete project preview.”

**Show:** growth plan page 1; point at the target student and three-channel funnel.

## 0:30-1:05 - Show the funnel and limits

“The base case is three hundred and sixty registrations from champion groups, eighty from separate club or placement-cell lists, and sixty from peer invitations. That adds to five hundred, but the conversion rates are assumptions, not evidence.

The budget is one thousand rupees for fixed champion delivery honoraria, three hundred for data, two hundred for creative support, and five hundred in reserve. I pay for approved delivery, not registrations. I would check overlap and conversion on day two.”

**Show:** Funnel lab base case. Select Lower-conversion case.

“Lower campus conversion takes the forecast to three hundred and sixty-five. Twelve extra groups could fill the gap, but the reserve pays for only ten extra champions. That forces a real choice: improve conversion, find volunteers, or revise the forecast.”

## 1:05-2:15 - Demonstrate the working asset

“I built Campus Relay because the difficult part is knowing which source needs attention today.

This dashboard starts with one hundred and sixty fictional registrations. The day selector shows the target and required daily pace. The champion toolkit generates a source-coded link and a WhatsApp message; it copies the message but sends nothing.

Now I register an invented final-year student using C zero one. The sample count increases once, and the student receives a personal referral link. I submit the same email using a different source. The count stays the same, and the original source keeps the credit.

I can use the student's code for a second sample student. That registration is attributed to peer referrals. The organizer can review source counts and export the simulated data as a CSV.

This is a browser-local prototype. It does not verify emails or sync registrations across devices. Those are the first production changes I would make.”

**Show:** Campaign pulse → Champion toolkit → Student flow. Use demo.one@example.com with C01, then the same email with CLUB01; use demo.two@example.com with the resulting referral code. Export CSV. Never enter real personal data.

## 2:15-3:00 - Judgment and learning

“My first idea was a referral tracker. The final solution adds registration, deduplication, attribution and a forecast because each one improves a daily campaign decision.

I rejected treating more groups as a guaranteed answer. It hides the access and budget problem. I also avoided cash per referral and mandatory sharing because the goal is relevant registrations, not artificial volume.

With another day, I would add shared storage, email verification and organizer access; confirm the actual workshop project and schedule; and prepare a small pilot to test the message.

My learning is that AI can produce a confident funnel very quickly. My job is to expose its assumptions, test the important ones, and ship something that helps act on the evidence.”

**Show:** Dashboard source counts; end on Funnel lab with overlap set to 10%.
